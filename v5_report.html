A payoff-reading modal agent that exploits, settles for fair, or gives in without rewarding the threat

V5 reads the payoff matrix and an outside random draw, and tries four things in order: exploit the opponent if provable, meet it at the Nash bargaining point, else read its commitment and give in with a probability that leaves the committer strictly below its fair payoff, else play maxmin. Against every pure commitment in 1,048 tests the committer never gets more than fair; copies reach the fair point in 200 of 200 random games; in the PD it reproduces PrudentBot exactly.

What was added to the repo

The original study evaluates idealised modal agents: formulas of provability logic ("play a if PA proves φ"), run exactly along the Kripke chain of GL, where level n sees every level below it and a boxed formula is true at level n iff it held at every lower level from its theory's index on. V4 was the best agent before: it reads payoffs, orders outcomes into cooperative / neutral / exploitative tiers, and probes the opponent against a constant exploiter, which reproduces PrudentBot in the PD. It is deterministic, picks outcomes by its own action ordering (so copies mis-coordinate in Battle of the Sexes), and has no concept of a threat.

Two files are new. rmodal.py extends the evaluator with randomness: a public draw r_pub both players see, and per player a coarse private draw r_c and a fine private draw r_f. A key is now (agent, opponent, role, r_pub, r_c, r_f), the agent's program at a key may depend only on its own draws, and conditions can quantify over the opponent's keys ("PA proves the opponent plays b at every value of its private draw" is a statement about its policy, which its source reveals). The whole system still has one fixed point on the chain. v5.py is the agent.

How V5 decides

For the key (r_pub, r_c, r_f), with u_F, v_F my and the opponent's payoffs at the fair point F, checks run in this order and the first that fires decides the action.

Ffair point

Discretised Nash bargaining solution: maximise (u − maxmin_me)(v − maxmin_opp) over the convex hull of pure outcomes, as a mix of at most two outcomes with weight a multiple of 1/NPUB, realised by r_pub. Both players compute the same F from the same matrix, which is the shared ordering the original README asked for. Ties among payoff-equal outcomes go to the actions that leave the other player least to gain by deviating.

Aexploit

For each opponent action b whose best response a pays me more than u_F, best first:

Con_{k+4} ∧ Box_k( opponent plays b at every key against Const_a ) → play a

PrudentBot's probe turned around. The implication "I play a → they play b" is not used: it passes vacuously whenever I don't play a, which is the level-1 artefact that made greedy modal UDT defect against FairBot.

Bfair
Box_0( I play F at all my keys → the opponent plays F at all its keys ) → play F(r_pub)

FairBot's test aimed at F. Löb's theorem gives mutual F between copies, and with a FairBot or PrudentBot aimed at F.

Cthreat

For each policy σ the opponent could have at this r_pub (with K = 1: "x when r_f < j else y", which is what a stone, a FairBot or a V5 in tier C plays):

Con_{k+3} ∧ Box_k( opponent plays σ at every (r_pub, r_c, r_f) ) → q = σ's action distribution; a* = best response to q; v_give = opp payoff under a* a_r = action minimising opp payoff against q; v_ref = opp payoff then if v_give ≤ v_F: play a* else: p = largest multiple of 1/M below (v_F − v_ref)/(v_give − v_ref) play a* when r_f < p·M, a_r otherwise

The rule from the threats paper: give in most of the time, but leave the committer strictly below its fair payoff in expectation, so committing was a mistake. This is the one tier that can take V5 below its own maxmin.

Dfloor

Maxmin action, ties to my F action.

Con_j = ¬Box_j(⊥) is false at levels ≤ j of the chain, so a check can only fire after its box has been read over several levels. Without this "patience", a copy's one-level fallback while it waits to read a constant bot was read as its policy, and copies in Battle of the Sexes exploited each other instead of meeting at F; tier A waits one level longer than tier C for the same reason. Checks are stratified over k = 1..6 so an opponent whose play settles only after a few levels (another V5 reading me) is still read.

  • Stone that never swerves: tier C reads it. Giving in (Sw) pays it 5, refusing (DS) pays it −100, so the threshold is 102/105 = 0.971; with M = 20, V5 swerves with p = 0.95. The stone's expectation is −0.25, below its fair 2 (it would get 5 if V5 always gave in). V5 expects −5.95, below its maxmin of −1: the price of giving in rather than crashing every time.
  • Stone that always swerves: tier A, V5 never swerves and takes 5.
  • Stone mixing 50/50 with its private draw (K = 4): tier C reads the mix; the stone expects −0.15. Always swerving would have handed it 2.5, above fair.
  • FairBot or PrudentBot aimed at F: V5 takes 5. Their fallback is to swerve, so against the constant non-swerver they swerve, and tier A reads that. V3-B and V4 exploit them the same way.

Ultimatum, in normal form

Offerer picks an offer o ∈ 0..10 to the responder; responder picks a threshold t and accepts iff o ≥ t. A mix over thresholds is any monotone acceptance rule, so the responder's probabilistic giving-in is representable. F = 5/5, and the tie-break picks "accept iff ≥ 5" over "accept everything" (same payoff at F, far less for the offerer to gain by deviating).

What this does not settle

  • The unexploitability notion ("committer's expectation ≤ its fair payoff") is the paper's; whether it is the right one when the committer is itself a reader is tested in one Ultimatum case only.
  • Reading an opponent's policy costs #actions² × M boxes per public draw. Private mixes finer than the coarse resolution K are not read and fall to the maxmin floor, which a finely mixing threatener can use (always-swerve hands a 50/50 Chicken mixer 2.5).
  • The patience constants (3 for tier C, 4 for tier A) are what made the named games work; they are tuned, not derived. Tier C's reading dynamics between two readers converged in every case tried, with no proof that they must.
  • Zero-sum games get a pure maxmin fallback, not the mixed one.
  • Idealised agents only. Nothing here is bounded; the repo's BPC bots are untouched.
